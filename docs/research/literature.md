# Literature review outline

The review should distinguish performance during assistance, calibration of a confidence judgment and transfer to an unaided task. The sources below are starting points for those arguments. Each proposed connection is a design choice to evaluate in this study.

| Review section | Source | Question to extract |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cognitive offloading | Risko & Gilbert (2016), *Cognitive Offloading*, Trends in Cognitive Sciences. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2016.07.002) | Which observable task choices could indicate use of an external aid, and what competing interpretations remain? |
| Confidence and monitoring | Fleming & Lau (2014), *How to measure metacognition*, Frontiers in Human Neuroscience. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2014.00443) | How do calibration, discrimination and metacognitive sensitivity differ, and which can the planned number of trials support? |
| Appropriate reliance | Lee & See (2004), *Trust in Automation: Designing for Appropriate Reliance*, Human Factors. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1518/hfes.46.1.50_30392) | How do properties of the aid, context and user affect reliance? Which constructs need self-report? |
| Interventions in AI-assisted decisions | Buçinca, Malaya & Gajos (2021), *To Trust or to Think: Cognitive Forcing Functions Can Reduce Overreliance on AI in AI-assisted Decision-making*, Proceedings of the ACM on Human-Computer Interaction. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3449287) | What did the manipulation require, who participated, what outcome was measured and when was AI accessible? |
| Probabilistic scoring | Brier (1950), *Verification of Forecasts Expressed in Terms of Probability*, Monthly Weather Review. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1175/1520-0493%281950%29078%3C0001%3AVOFEIT%3E2.0.CO%3B2) | When is a binary event and its probability elicited unambiguously? |

For a full review, search ACM Digital Library, PsycINFO and relevant citation chains using combinations of “AI-assisted decision making”, “cognitive forcing”, “confidence calibration”, “transfer” and “unaided”. Log the search date, query, inclusion rules and screening decisions. Extract the study population, randomization, AI correctness, outcome timing, measurement scale and attrition. Distinguish replication of an observed result from transfer to a new population or task.
